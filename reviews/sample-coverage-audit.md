## Claims retained for verification, not quietly discarded or endorsed

The original lesson listed caffeine solubility as about **20 g/L at 20°C**, **108 g/L at 60°C**, and hundreds of g/L near boiling; L-theanine as **350 g/L at 25°C**; EGCG as **33.8 g/L at 80°C**; and instability above **pH 6.5**. It attributed these to the Spiro, Keenan, and Perva-Uzunalić sources. This review has not established those exact values in those sources. They remain recoverable here and in the original file, but are not repeated as verified constants in the lesson.

Also held back as general rules: caffeine/theanine always preceding EGCG; a universal below-80°C EGCG-to-caffeine shift; a guaranteed calmer-focus benefit from a full steep; shaded-tea rankings inferred from Keenan; a guaranteed late-steep amino-acid/aroma progression; and cold brewing for 12–24 hours reliably matching or exceeding a hot cup. These are evidence questions, not cuts made merely because the material is technical.

The original Spiro–Jago entry read “Kinetics and equilibria of tea infusion: Kinetics of extraction of caffeine and theaflavins,” *Journal of the Science of Food and Agriculture*, 33(4), 333–340 (1982). The verified paper by those authors is “Part 3.—Rotating-disc experiments interpreted by a steady-state model,” *Journal of the Chemical Society, Faraday Transactions 1*, 78, 295–305, DOI **10.1039/F19827800295**. The Spiro–Siddique title is likewise restored to its full published wording.
